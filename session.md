Based on the overview alone, the match is very good, though "hopeful" is inferred from the arc's framing rather than confirmed by an explicit description of the film's mood.

## Safe Pick: My Hero Academia: Heroes Rising (2019)

Based on your stated preferences for **hopeful** and **superhero** works, **My Hero Academia: Heroes Rising** is a strong match — and I'd recommend it.

**Why it matches:**
- **Superhero**: The premise is squarely superhero fiction — students of a hero academy (Class 1-A) taking on real hero work, protecting citizens, and fighting a villain with a powerful Quirk. It centers on Deku and his classmates as the *next generation* of heroes.
- **Hopeful**: The overview explicitly frames them as "the island's only hope" after All Might's retirement, and the tone leans on the inspiring idea that a new generation rises to protect people. The peaceful, vacation-like setting before the attack also reinforces that aspirational, earnest mood.

**Caveat worth noting:** The villain attack and the "unfathomable" Quirk introduce a threat that may feel more intense/dramatic than pure lightheartedness, but the story's framing (young heroes as the answer, citizens saved) keeps it squarely within a hopeful superhero spirit.

So if you want an energetic superhero story with young heroes stepping up to save the day, this fits your brief well.

## Wildcard Pick: About Time (2013)

I'd recommend **About Time** — with one clear caveat.

**Matches (hopeful):** The overview is saturated with the hopeful tone you want. It explicitly frames the journey as "a lesson in learning to appreciate life itself as it is, as it comes," and the most important takeaway is valuing "the people living alongside us." That's a warm, life-affirming, sentimental direction rather than a cynical one. The time-travel ability is used not to fix a broken world but to let a man cherish the ordinary moments of his life, which reads as genuinely uplifting.

**Mismatches (superhero):** This is where the evidence is weak and I'd flag it plainly. There are no superheroes here — no capes, no villains, no high-stakes save-the-city action. The father's gift is framed as a personal, domestic power ("they can change what happens and has happened in their own lives"), not a superpower used for heroism. If you specifically want the superhero genre (costumes, epic conflict, action set pieces), this will disappoint. The only element that brushes against your request is the literal "ability to travel through time," which is a fantastical ability — but it's used for romance and family drama, not heroics.

**Bottom line:** pick this for a hopeful, heartfelt, life-appreciating watch; skip it if the superhero element is a hard requirement rather than a "nice to have."

*Available on: Netflix, Netflix Standard with Ads (not a live availability guarantee)*
